Wenhao Peng received the Ph.D. degree in electrical and computer engineering and the M.S.E. degree in mechanical engineering from the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor, MI, USA, in 2026 and 2024, respectively. He received the M.S. degree in electrical and computer engineering from the University of Michigan in 2019 and the B.S.E. degrees from the University of Michigan and Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Shanghai, China, in 2018. His published modeling work connects thermodynamics and mechanical vibrations with resonator and filter design. In ferroelectric BAW technology, he developed a physics based equivalent circuit model that bridges nonlinear ferroelectric thermodynamics and resonator DC bias dependent response. In piezoelectric BAW technology, he derived an energy based expression for electromechanical coupling as the ratio of converted electrical energy to supplied elastic energy and proved by modal analysis its consistency with the coefficient determined from the series and parallel resonance frequencies when the mode is sufficiently isolated from other modes, making modal field distributions useful for evaluating layer stacks and device performance at mm-Waves. Experimentally, he demonstrated the first periodically poled AlN/ScAlN/AlN trilayer FBAR operating above 50 GHz, a quad band switchless filter bank, an acoustic phase inversion switch, and an intrinsically switchable balanced filter. He has published in IEEE T-MTT, IEEE MWTL, IEEE MWCL, Applied Physics Letters, has presented as the first author at IEEE/MTT-S International Microwave Symposium in 2021, 2024, and 2025, and serves as a peer reviewer for leading journals in IEEE, APS, and AIP.

mm-Wave acoustic resonators based on periodically poled III-nitride thin films
Jan 2022 - Dec 2025
Demonstrated the first periodically poled AlN/ScAlN/AlN trilayer FBARs at 56 GHz, followed by a second generation 36 GHz resonator with a redesigned electrode and layer stack.
Derived an energy based expression for electromechanical coupling from the open circuit eigenmodes of an FBAR. It evaluates the coupling coefficient as the ratio of converted electrical energy to supplied elastic energy, which is proved by modal analysis to be consistent with the value determined from the series and parallel resonance frequencies. The expression enabled direct evaluation of layer contributions to the energy terms and guided electrode and layer stack selection for the mm-Wave FBAR work.
Advanced the nanofabrication and pulsed-field polarization switching processes of MBE grown AlN/ScAlN multilayer structures.
Performed mm-Wave characterization up to 67 GHz with on-wafer Thu Reflect Line and open short de-embedding structures validated in ADS Momentum and Ansys HFSS.

Reconfigurable RF frontends based on intrinsically switchable BST acoustic resonators
Sep 2019 - Feb 2025
Developed the first equivalent circuit model of ferroelectric driven BAW resonators based on ferroelectric thermodynamics. Applied analytical dynamics to bridge ferroelectric thermodynamics and acoustic resonance. Validated the model by direct comparison with BST FBAR measurements across DC bias.
Demonstrated the first switchless quad band BST FBAR filter bank and the first intrinsically switchable balanced ferroelectric FBAR filter at 2 GHz.
Demonstrated the first RF acoustic phase inversion switch based on poled ferroelectric stacked crystal filters.
Synthesized filters using the image parameter method, applying Mason and mBVD resonator models, and completed cleanroom fabrication and DC bias dependent RF characterization.
